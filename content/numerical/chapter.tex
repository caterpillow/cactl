  \subsection{Numerical integration}
	Simpson (\texttt{Integrate.h}): fixed $2n$ slices, error $O(h^4 f^{(4)})$, exact for cubics; double $n$ until stable.
	Adaptive Simpson (\texttt{IntegrateAdaptive.h}): \texttt{eps} is the absolute error budget of the whole interval (halves get \texttt{eps/2}); it stops splitting below width $10^{-10}$, so rescale tiny domains, and scale \texttt{eps} by the answer's magnitude for relative precision.
	Gauss--Legendre (\texttt{GaussLegendre.h}): $n$ nodes at the roots of $P_n$, weights $2/((1-x_i^2)P_n'(x_i)^2)$, exact for polynomials of degree $< 2n$; far more accurate per evaluation on smooth integrands, useless across kinks. \\
	Tips: split at kinks, discontinuities and (oscillatory) periods so each piece is smooth. Endpoint singularity at $a$: substitute $x = a + t^2$, $dx = 2t\,dt$. Infinite range: $x = \tan t$ on $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$, or $x = t/(1-t)$ on $[0,1)$. Flatten an integrand with very different scales by a change of variable (e.g. $x = e^u$ for $\int f(x)\,dx/x$). Nested integrals cost $\texttt{eps}^{-d}$: loosen the inner \texttt{eps}. Use \texttt{long double}.
